All three clients were run under identical conditions in one sequential campaign on 2026-10-11 (08:19–11:40 local time). Findings and caveats:

**Read before quoting numbers**

* **Small samples.** Downloads: Debian 2 runs per client, Arch 1 run per client. Scale: N=50 and N=200 one run per client, N=500 two runs per client. Hot: one run (10 rounds) per client. Treat differences below roughly 20 % as noise. Arch and Ubuntu runs per client were limited by the 15 GB budget (9.8 GB used by downloads; a third client made a larger ISO per client impossible: 3 × 2.9 GB would exceed the budget). The Ubuntu 26.04.1 server and Fedora 44 Workstation torrents are in `bench/torrents/` and can be added with `--plan debian:2,arch:1,ubuntu:1` when budget allows.
* **Network.** Link capability is about 35 MB/s (281 Mbit/s) by Cloudflare and 29–33 MB/s from the Debian mirror at the time of the campaign. An earlier manual 100 MB curl from the same mirror host (saimei.ftp.acc.umu.se) at 07:55 managed only 1.36 MB/s, so mirror/swarm conditions vary a lot over the day. The swarms saturated roughly 60 MB/s peaks for qBittorrent and TorrentRS, i.e. the downloads are bounded by the link and swarm, not by the client CPU. The first Cloudflare probe (100 MB) failed with an HTTP error and is excluded from the table.
* **Machine load.** Other agents were compiling during the whole campaign (1-min load average at run start: 2.7 to 11.0, see tables). CPU-time figures are user+sys of the client process only and are less sensitive to this than wall-clock rates, but the Transmission download times in particular may include scheduling noise.
* **Transmission does not hash pre-existing complete data.** In the scale test Transmission reports all 500 torrents seeding after 2 s with 0.4 CPU-seconds, so it trusted the existing files (no verification), while qBittorrent verified all of them (14.9 CPU-seconds, 504 s for N=500 because libtorrent checks one torrent at a time) and TorrentRS verified them in 18–24 s (about 3.6 CPU-seconds). "Time to all seeding" is therefore not a like-for-like comparison. Steady-state idle numbers are unaffected.
* **RSS vs footprint.** libtorrent (qBittorrent) memory-maps the files: after verification or while downloading, `ps` RSS shows hundreds of MiB of file-backed pages (799 MiB Debian, 1.6 GiB Arch) while `phys_footprint` is only 31–46 MiB. Transmission shows the opposite pattern while moving data: footprint 562 MiB during the Arch download and 696 MiB (still 678 MiB 20 s later) while serving three local leechers, versus about 50 MiB for qBittorrent. Footprint is the SPEC §2 metric, so the tables lead with it; the RSS columns follow the brief (`ps -o rss`).
* **"Marginal cost per idle torrent" is approximate.** It is the difference between the N=50 (single run) and N=500 footprints divided by 450. At idle the footprints are 4–40 MiB so allocator granularity and run-to-run variation (±1–2 MiB) are comparable to the effect; the RSS variant is unreliable (RSS drops during the idle window as file-backed pages are evicted, giving the negative value for qBittorrent). TorrentRS shows about 13 KiB/torrent of footprint, which is above the 4 KB SPEC target at this measurement precision.
* **Wake-ups.** `Idle wake-ups/s` is `ri_pkg_idle_wkups` (what `powermetrics` reports as wake-ups and what SPEC §2 budgets at <= 1/s). `Interrupt wake-ups/s` are timer interrupts that did not leave an idle package state: Transmission shows a constant 32.7/s, identical across all N (a fixed timer, not load-dependent), qBittorrent 3.6–6.6/s and TorrentRS 2.5–6.4/s. `top` IDLEW is a 20 s window after the 10 min idle window and is noisier. Idle CPU for all three clients is below 0.25 %; at N=500 TorrentRS used 0.20 % vs 0.08 % for qBittorrent and Transmission, the opposite of the memory result.
* **Announces.** No client sent periodic announces during the 10 min idle windows (interval 1800 s). Transmission and TorrentRS rate-limit their initial `started` announces (about 60 per minute), so a part of the `started` announces for large N lands after t0; that is start-up, not idle traffic. qBittorrent sent all of them before the torrents finished verifying. At shutdown each client sent `stopped` for every torrent.
* **Hot variant.** Leechers are 3 Transmission 4.1.3 processes per round in all cases, so the seeder is the only variable. qBittorrent needed `Session\MultiConnectionsPerIp=true` for local mode (libtorrent refuses several connections from one IP by default and the leechers all share the seeder's address). Round 8 of the qBittorrent run took 33 s instead of 5 s (a stall, kept in the data). Upload throughput is bounded by the Transmission leechers on loopback, not necessarily by the seeder.
* **TorrentRS hot run failed twice and is excluded (two entries under "Failed runs").** Reproducible with `bench/scripts/tests/repro-seeder-reconnect.py` (about 2 minutes): a TorrentRS seeder serves a first leecher process (35 MB of data complete after 34–41 s, i.e. a slow first unchoke), but when that leecher process exits and a new one from the same IP connects, the new one never gets a connection (seeder reports 0 peers, no data in 100 s). In the full hot run round 1 completes in 9–12 s and round 2 sees no progress for 60 s, both attempts. Transmission and qBittorrent seeders handle the same sequence.
* **TorrentRS download peers.** TorrentRS connected to far fewer peers than the others (median 12–37 vs about 100 for Transmission and 69–94 for qBittorrent) and still matched qBittorrent's speed; it uses 2.7 CPU-seconds for the Debian ISO against 10–16 for the others.
* **External interference.** One early smoke run was lost when its Transmission leecher daemons were terminated by something outside the harness (another agent's activity on the shared machine). The harness now detects dead leechers and retries a failed run once; no retried run is part of the tables except as listed under "Failed runs".
